A four-period flow

  1. Cone and degenerate sections. Tilt the plane, name each curve, then move the plane to the vertex.
  2. Circle and parabola. Build each equation from its definition.
  3. Ellipse and hyperbola. Foci, eccentricity, latus rectum.
  4. Applications, summary sheet and the quiz.

Where the names come from

Apollonius (262 B.C. to 190 B.C.) named the parabola and the hyperbola. In the textbook, para means “for” and bola means “throwing”: the shape of a ball thrown in the air.

    EllipsePlanets travel on ellipses with the Sun at one focus. In an elliptical whispering gallery, a whisper at one focus is heard at the other.
    ParabolaHeadlights, flashlights, satellite dishes and telescope mirrors use the reflecting property: rays from the focus leave parallel, and parallel rays gather at the focus.
    HyperbolaCooling towers are built with hyperbolic cross-sections. Radio navigation fixes a position from the difference in arrival times of two signals.
    CircleWheels, gears and the ripples from a dropped pebble. Every point of a ripple front is the same distance from where the pebble fell.
