## Where the names come from

Apollonius (262 B.C. to 190 B.C.) named the parabola and the hyperbola. In the textbook, *para* means “for” and *bola* means “throwing”: the shape of a ball thrown in the air.

# The four conics side by side

|  | Circle | Parabola | Ellipse | Hyperbola |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Plane angle β | β = 90° | β = α | α \< β \< 90° | 0 ≤ β \< α |
| Definition | Points equidistant from a fixed point, the centre | Points equidistant from a fixed point, the focus, and a fixed line, the directrix | Sum of distances from two fixed points, the foci, is constant | Difference of distances from two fixed points, the foci, is constant |
| Standard equation | (*x* − *h*)² + (*y* − *k*)² = *r*² | *y*² = 4*ax* *x*² = 4*ay* | *x*²*a*² + *y*²*b*² = 1 | *x*²*a*² − *y*²*b*² = 1 |
| Focus or foci | none; centre (*h*, *k*) | (*a*, 0) | (± *c*, 0) | (± *c*, 0) |
| Directrix | none | *x* = −*a* | none in this chapter | none in this chapter |
| Key relation | distance to centre = *r* | PF = PB | *c*² = *a*² − *b*² | *c*² = *a*² + *b*² |
| Eccentricity | 0 | 1 | *e* = *c*/*a*, less than 1 | *e* = *c*/*a*, at least 1 |
| Latus rectum | none | 4*a* | 2*b*²/*a* | 2*b*²/*a* |
| Axes | none | one axis of symmetry | major 2*a*, minor 2*b* | transverse 2*a*, conjugate 2*b* |

**Ellipse**Planets travel on ellipses with the Sun at one focus. In an elliptical whispering gallery, a whisper at one focus is heard at the other.

**Parabola**Headlights, flashlights, satellite dishes and telescope mirrors use the reflecting property: rays from the focus leave parallel, and parallel rays gather at the focus.

**Hyperbola**Cooling towers are built with hyperbolic cross-sections. Radio navigation fixes a position from the difference in arrival times of two signals.

**Circle**Wheels, gears and the ripples from a dropped pebble. Every point of a ripple front is the same distance from where the pebble fell.
